# GUIDE: FRETish (NASA FRET structured natural language)

SPEC_STATUS: partial
SPEC_SOURCES (NASA publications read in this project; the complete FRETish grammar was NOT obtained):
- NASA/TM-20220000049 (Integrating FRET with Copilot), section 2.2: a FRETish requirement has up to six fields:
  scope, condition, component*, shall*, timing, response* (* = mandatory).
- NASA/TM-20220007510 (Realizability Checking of Requirements in FRET): FRETish does not allow arbitrary nesting of temporal
  operators; table 3 and 4 print natural language next to FRETish.
- "The Ten Lockheed Martin Cyber-Physical Challenges" (RE 2020, NTRS 20205000433): semantics of triggers, versions v1 to v4 of one requirement.
- "Let's speak FRETish" (NTRS 20240004425, slides): lists of scope and timing keywords.
- NASA/TM-2021-20210013008 (liquid mixer, Table 2.1) and NASA/TM-20240006865 (Tables 1 to 4): more pairs of natural language and FRETish.
- NASA/TM-20240006865 and the Lift plus Cruise case study (NTRS 20220017032): timing keyword usage.

## Shape of a requirement

[scope] [condition] component shall [timing] satisfy response

Examples (the second, third and sixth were rewritten for this bench so that no example equals a reference sentence of the test data; the others are as printed in NASA reports):
  when liquid_level_1 the liquid_mixer shall immediately satisfy ! valve_0
  controller shall always satisfy (armed & !fault) => valve_open
  in landing mode gear_ctrl shall always satisfy gear_down
  in flight mode if airspeed < 100 the aircraft shall within 10 seconds satisfy (airspeed >= 100)
  when liquid_level_1 the liquid_mixer shall until emergency_button satisfy if ! liquid_level_2 then valve_1
  if heater_on the thermostat shall always satisfy temp_ok

## Fields

- scope (optional): states in which the requirement applies. Keywords listed in the slides: in, before, after, notin, onlyIn, onlyBefore, onlyAfter;
  no scope means global. A mode scope is written like "in roll hold mode".
  Source (Adam et al. 2023, FRETish description): FRETish has 8 scopes (global, in, before, after, notin, only in, only before, only after). In place of a single
  Boolean variable a scope may take a Boolean expression; for "in" the expression form is written with "while", for example "while vehicle_mode = hover".
  UNVERIFIED addition (v2): "after landed & powerOff" and "before taxiing & receivedClearance" were read from workshop slides through a summary tool.
- condition (optional): a Boolean expression introduced by a keyword. There are two kinds (FRET repository, fret-electron/docs/_media/user-interface/examples/condition.md):
  "a regular condition, meaning the requirement holds upon a condition becoming true; i.e., at the point where the condition becomes true after being false,
  or the condition is true at the beginning of a scope interval" and "a holding condition, meaning the response holds whenever the condition holds."
  "A holding condition is introduced by the keyword whenever. A regular condition is introduced by the keyword upon (or when, where, if)."
  Source (Adam et al. 2023): upon, when and if are synonymous in FRETish; "unless" means the same as "when !".
  Source (Hejase et al., project file "main2.pdf"): a regular condition "triggers the response to occur at the time the expression's value becomes true, or is true at
  the beginning of the scope interval"; with whenever it "triggers the response to occur every time the expression's value is true".
  Source (FRET repository, Requirement.g4): "when in <mode>" and "if in <mode>" are parsed as a SCOPE, not as a condition.
  Semantics (RE 2020 paper): a regular condition is a TRIGGER. The paper writes "the response must hold at every time point where the expression is true" without a
  condition as: "component shall always satisfy (expression) => response".
  The official parser of this bench was also checked: when, if and upon give the identical logical formula for the same expression; whenever gives a different one.
- component (mandatory): the name of the component the requirement is about, written as one identifier or "the <identifier>".
- shall (mandatory keyword).
- timing (optional): always, never, eventually, immediately, next, within <N> <unit>, for <N> <unit>, after <N> <unit>, until <condition>, before <condition>.
  Also seen in the reports: "every <N> ticks" and "with probability <= <p>" (extensions used in NASA/TM-20240006865).
  The slides list: always, never, eventually, immediately, for, within, after, until, before.
  Source (Adam et al. 2023): the 10 timings are immediately, at the next timepoint, always, eventually, never, for N time steps, within N time steps,
  after N time steps, until <bool_expr>, before <bool_expr>.
  Source (Adam et al. 2023): when the scope is omitted it is global, when the condition is omitted it is true, and when the timing is omitted it is "eventually".
  Source (Adam et al. 2023): FRETish does not allow arbitrary nesting of temporal operators and cannot express timed operators with a lower bound other than 0.
- response (mandatory): the keyword "satisfy" followed by a Boolean expression.
  Operators seen in the reports: & | ! => = < <= > >= + - * /. Boolean connectives and arithmetic relations work over integer and real variables.
  Functions: absReal(x) for the absolute value (the official parser rejects abs). preInt(init, n) and preReal(init, n) give the value of n at the previous
  time point, or init at the start of the trace (Adam et al. 2023). persisted(n, p) is true at the time point where p has held in the previous n time steps
  and also holds now, n+1 time steps in total, a duration of n time units (FSM requirement templates paper, project file "main2.pdf").
  A response of the form "if A then B" appears in the liquid mixer pairs.

## Naming

Variables and components are written as identifiers without spaces, for example liquid_level_1, valve_0, emergency_button, ap_standby_state, FSM.
The reports contain no rule for choosing variable names from natural language words. Words given in parentheses in a natural language requirement
(for example "(not standby)") are variable names in the reports.

## Not known from the sources (the test bench adds NO rule here)

The reserved words and the full grammar, how "while" in natural language maps to scope or condition, how a negated state ("while not X") is written,
and when to split one natural language requirement into several FRETish requirements.

# TEMPLATE: Boilerplates (Hull, Jackson and Dick; DODT style clause set)

SPEC_STATUS: partial
SPEC_SOURCES:
- Grosser et al. 2023, "A Comparative Evaluation of Requirement Template Systems", section III and Table I (project PDF). It says:
  Boilerplates [Hull, Jackson, Dick 2005, pp. 81ff] are a set of templates for different types of requirements. Stakeholder
  requirement: "The <stakeholder type> shall be able to <capability>." System requirement: "The <system> shall <function> <object>."

  recombined. The DODT framework presents a consolidated set of all main and sub-clauses.

  with the structure "While <state>, <system> shall have <parameter> of at most <quantity> <unit>."

## Base boilerplates of Sect. 4.8 of the book (verified)

Capability (stakeholder requirement):
  The <stakeholder type> shall be able to <capability>.
  The <stakeholder type> shall be able to <capability> within <performance> of <event> while <operational condition>.
  E.g. The weapons operator shall be able to fire a missile within 3 seconds of radar sighting while in severe sea conditions.
Constraint (stakeholder requirement):
  The <stakeholder> shall not be placed in breach of <applicable law>.
  E.g. The ambulance driver shall not be placed in breach of national road regulations.
System requirement with a capacity performance:
  The <system> shall <function> not less than <quantity> <object> while <operational condition>.
  E.g. The communications system shall sustain telephone contact with not less than 10 callers while in the absence of external power.
Periodicity:
  The <system> shall <function> <object> every <performance> <units>.
The book also says "shall" marks a requirement, and some approaches use "shall", "should" and "may" for different priorities.

## Table 4.4 of the book (verified, constraint boilerplates by type of constraint)

Performance/capability:   The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> not less than <performance> times per <units>.
Performance/capability:   The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> of type <qualification> within <performance> <units>.
Performance/capacity:     The <system> shall be able to <function> not less than <quantity> <object>.
Performance/timeliness:   The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> within <performance> <units> from <event>.
Performance/periodicity:  The <system> shall be able to <function> not less than <quantity> <object> within <performance> <units>.
Interoperability/capacity: The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> composed of not less than <performance> <units> with <external entity>.
Sustainability/periodicity: The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> for <performance> <units> every <performance> <units>.
Environmental/operability: The <system> shall be able to <function> <object> while <operational condition>.
Periodicity (Sect. 4.8):  The <system> shall <function> <object> every <performance> <units>.
The book says only the parts in bold are relevant to the constraint, and that several boilerplates may express a similar constraint.

## Forms that can be used (verified or secondary, see above)

Main clauses:
  The <stakeholder type> shall be able to <capability>.
  The <system> shall <function> <object>.
  <system> shall have <parameter> of at most <quantity> <unit>.
  <subject> shall [not] <action>
  <subject> shall [not] be able to <action>
Condition prefix (comma after the prefix):
  While <state>, <main clause>
  If <state or event>, <main clause>
  Unless <state or event>, <main clause>
Suffix:
  <main clause> after <event> | before <event> | using <entity>

## Combination (DODT 2011, verified)

Boilerplates may be concatenated: a sentence can be built from several boilerplates in sequence (main clause followed by condition or constraint clauses).
The book's own examples append "within <performance> of <event>" and "while <operational condition>" to a main clause.
